Research

Disagreement as signal, not noise.

The standard NLP pipeline launders disagreement: collect labels, take a majority vote, train on the winner, report accuracy. For many tasks that's fine. For the tasks I care about — is this argument fallacious? is this answer different from that one? is this persuasive, and to whom? — the vote erases exactly the part worth studying. My research treats human label variation as something to model, predict, and evaluate against.

I work on this at three levels: data — annotation protocols and decision-tree guidelines that expose where trained annotators diverge, rather than papering over it; modeling — training-free methods for getting calibrated soft label distributions out of LLMs; and evaluation — metrics that ask whether model outputs are diverse and faithful in ways embedding similarity can't see. Most of my experiments run on UNC's GPU cluster, serving open models at scale with vLLM.

Current threads

Computational argumentation

Detecting fallacies and assessing argument quality in real discourse, where the boundary between a rhetorical move and a fallacy is itself contested. I build annotation protocols for slippery categories like whataboutism and false dilemma — including multi-question decision trees that turn one hard judgment into several easier ones — and study inter-annotator agreement to find out where, and why, trained annotators diverge.

status: active · annotation studies + LLM evaluation in progress

Human label variation

When annotators disagree, the distribution of labels carries information a majority vote throws away. I work on training-free methods for predicting soft labels in fallacy classification — logit extraction, verbalized distributions, self-consistency sampling — in the spirit of perspectivist NLP: evaluating models against the full distribution of human judgments rather than a single gold answer.

status: active · paper in preparation

LLM evaluation & semantic diversity

Embedding similarity is a blunt instrument for asking whether two model responses actually differ. I'm developing structured axes of comparison — conceptual anchor, reasoning structure, perspective, grounding, scope, register, implicature — to measure the diversity of what models say across samples, not just how they phrase it, and to study homogenization across model families.

status: active

Agents & planning

How language models and reasoning models can divide cognitive labor. LRPlan pairs them as collaborating agents for planning under implicit and explicit constraints, trading accuracy against inference cost — state of the art on TravelPlanner and TimeArena-Static.

representative: LRPlan, Findings of EMNLP 2025

Where this is heading

Three questions I want a PhD's worth of time for. First: if models can predict label distributions, can they be trained and evaluated perspectivally end-to-end — keeping whose-judgment-it-is as a first-class variable instead of collapsing it? Second: can disagreement itself become a training signal — telling a model not just what the answer is, but how contested it is? Third: can argument-quality models give feedback that actually helps people write and reason better, rather than just scoring them?

Projects & infrastructure

Fallacy annotation tooling

react · annotation interfaces · 2025–present

Interactive interfaces for whataboutism and false-dilemma labeling, with seeded shuffling for assignment balance and guided multi-question decision trees that decompose each judgment. Built to make disagreement visible and analyzable rather than something to adjudicate away.

Inter-annotator agreement pipelines

python · analysis · 2025–present

Analysis pipelines for multi-annotator fallacy data: pairwise Cohen's κ, per-question disagreement breakdowns, and evaluation of LLM prompting strategies (definitions, decision trees, single- and multi-turn) against both majority labels and full label distributions.

Evaluation infrastructure

vllm · slurm · 2025–present

Multi-model serving setups on a SLURM-managed GPU cluster — concurrent vLLM endpoints for generation and embedding models — supporting panel-style evaluation of fine-tuned checkpoints against open baselines.
